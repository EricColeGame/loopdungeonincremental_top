## How the Loop Dungeon Incremental Prestige Reset Actually Works

The Loop Dungeon Incremental prestige reset follows the classic incremental bargain: you give up a run you have already solved, and you keep the compounding bonuses that run produced. The store description puts it plainly — even after a reset, progress continues through prestige bonuses that help your party reach deeper floors and unlock new opportunities. That is the entire engine. Every expedition should leave you slightly stronger than the last one, even when the floor counter drops back to one.

| Run phase | What it looks like | What to do | Prestige ready? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening run | Heroes die quickly on unfamiliar floors | Recruit, test roles, buy cheap upgrades | No |
| Mid run | Floors fall steadily and loot flows | Push until clear speed visibly slows | Start tracking |
| Plateau | New floors drag, upgrade costs outpace income | Spend what you have, note your wall floor | Yes |
| Post-prestige | Old walls fall fast again | Push past your previous best, then repeat | Cycle restarts |

### What carries over and what does not

Permanent prestige bonuses and account-level progress survive. Run-level loot, current floor depth, and temporary buffs do not. Always read the confirmation screen before you accept a reset, because the exact list can shift between patches and the in-game prompt is the only authority that matters.

- **Permanent bonuses:** the core of the Loop Dungeon Incremental prestige system, and the reason to reset at all.
- **Achievements and cloud saves:** tied to your Steam account, so they are unaffected by a reset.
- **Run-level resources:** spent, lost, or converted — check the prompt rather than guessing.
- **Party roster:** verify in-game whether recruits persist, since incremental RPGs handle this differently.

## When to Prestige in Loop Dungeon Incremental

Timing is the biggest lever in Loop Dungeon Incremental prestige, and it is also where most players guess. You do not need a perfect formula. You need three honest measurements: how long a full loop takes, how much a full loop earns, and what the next meaningful upgrade costs. When the third number dwarfs the second, the run is done.

| Signal you are seeing | What it usually means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Floors you cleared easily now take several loops | Current permanent bonuses have capped out | Spend remaining currency, then prestige |
| Next upgrade costs more than a full loop earns | Marginal return has collapsed | Bank one more loop, then reset |
| Party wipes on the same floor repeatedly | Composition problem, not raw power | Swap roles and retest before resetting |
| Combat runs on autopilot with no decisions left | You have hit the optimization ceiling | Prestige and reinvest |
| A patch changed costs or hero balance | Old assumptions may be stale | Re-test your wall floor before committing |

Two failure modes cost players the most. Resetting too early leaves currency and unlocks on the table, which means your next run starts weaker than it should. Resetting too late is worse: slow loops quietly hide a stalled multiplier, and hours of play produce almost nothing. If you are unsure, clear your wall floor twice in a row. If the second clear is not faster than the first, you already have your answer.

## Permanent Upgrade Priorities and Party Planning

Every Loop Dungeon Incremental prestige cycle should start with a spending plan, not a shopping spree. Early prestige currency compounds hardest when it shortens loop time, because faster loops generate more currency, which buys more speed. Survivability comes second — it only becomes the priority when wipes, rather than slow clears, are what end your runs.

| Priority | Upgrade focus | Why it pays back | Best timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Damage and clear speed | Shortens every future loop, which compounds | First few prestiges |
| 2 | Survivability and healing | Stops wall wipes that waste entire loops | When wipes become the bottleneck |
| 3 | Loot and currency gain | Multiplies income per hour of play | After loops are fast and stable |
| 4 | Utility and quality of life | Saves real time on repetitive tasks | Steady background investment |

### Party roles worth testing each cycle

| Role | Job in the loop | What to test each cycle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frontline / tank | Soaks damage so backline heroes keep dealing | Whether one tank is enough at your new depth |
| Damage dealer | Converts survivability into clear speed | Which damage hero scales best with your current bonuses |
| Support / healer | Extends runs and prevents wipes | Whether healing beats raw mitigation at your wall |
| Utility / flex | Fills gaps in synergy | One swap per cycle to find better combinations |

Change one variable at a time. Run the same floor three times with a new hero or upgrade, compare clear times, and keep the change only if the number improves. This is slow, unglamorous, and far more reliable than rebuilding the whole party on a hunch.

| Prestige cycle | Main goal | Where to focus | Move on when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Learn the reset screen | Cheap damage upgrades | You clear your old wall in one loop |
| 2–3 | Build clear speed | Damage and loop time | Floors fall noticeably faster than last cycle |
| 4–5 | Stabilize the squad | Survivability and healing | Wipes stop ending runs |

## Prestige FAQ

**How often should I prestige in Loop Dungeon Incremental?**
There is no fixed schedule. Reset when a run stops producing measurable progress: new floors take far longer, and the next upgrade costs more than a full loop earns. Early on, that may mean resetting every couple of hours; later, your wall floor will take longer to reach. Treat your own clear times as the benchmark, not someone else's timetable.

**Do I lose my heroes or achievements when I reset?**
Achievements and cloud saves are tied to your Steam account and are unaffected. Permanent prestige bonuses are designed to persist through resets. The party roster is the one detail worth verifying in-game, because the reset confirmation screen lists exactly what carries over and that list can change between patches.

**Is Loop Dungeon Incremental prestige worth it if I only play passively?**
Yes, with a caveat. Automatic combat means a passive player still collects loot and levels heroes, but the reset decision is the one moment that genuinely rewards attention. A five-minute review at each prestige — check loop time, check upgrade costs, spend deliberately — is usually enough to keep a passive account moving.
