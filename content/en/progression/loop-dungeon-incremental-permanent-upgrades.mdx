## How Loops, Resets, and Prestige Bonuses Actually Work

The core cycle is simple to describe and surprisingly deep to optimize:

1. **Send your party into a dungeon loop.** Combat runs automatically while your heroes clear floors and pick up loot.
2. **Bank resources.** Every expedition feeds your long-term growth, even the ones that end badly.
3. **Invest in permanent improvements.** These are the upgrades that persist beyond the current run.
4. **Reset when progress slows.** You restart shallower, but prestige bonuses make the next climb faster.
5. **Repeat deeper.** Each loop should end further than the last.

The store page is explicit that even after a reset, your progress continues through prestige bonuses. That is the engine of the whole game — the reset is not a punishment, it is a purchase.

Here is the general shape of what survives a reset versus what does not. Exact labels and rules are spelled out in-game, so treat this as the pattern rather than a patch note.

| Element | Carries over after reset? | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Permanent upgrades | Yes | The core of your long-term power curve |
| Prestige bonuses | Yes | Multiply the speed of every future run |
| Unlocked heroes and upgrade paths | Yes | Expands your strategic options permanently |
| Current floor depth | No | You restart shallower, but climb faster |
| Run-based hero levels | No | Temporary power; expect to lose it |
| Loot gathered this run | Converted or spent | Feeds permanent growth before the reset |

The practical takeaway: anything temporary should be squeezed for value *before* you reset. Anything permanent should be bought as early as it becomes affordable.

## Which Permanent Upgrades to Prioritize First

New players tend to spread currency across every option on the board. That is the slowest possible path. Loop Dungeon Incremental permanent upgrades reward focus — a few well-stacked multipliers will outpace a dozen scattered minor purchases every time.

Use this priority ladder as a starting framework:

| Priority | Upgrade type | Buy when | Why it comes first |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1 | Core damage and survivability multipliers | First two or three resets | Faster clears mean faster income for everything else |
| Tier 1 | Resource and loot gain | Immediately after your first clear speed bump | Compounding income beats raw stats early |
| Tier 2 | Party size, slots, or recruitment options | Once clears are stable and consistent | More heroes multiply every other upgrade you own |
| Tier 2 | Offline and idle efficiency | If you play in short sessions or passively | Converts real time into progression |
| Tier 3 | Deep unlock paths and late-game branches | After prestige bonuses are stacking | Expensive, but opens strategies the early tree cannot reach |

A simple rule of thumb: if an upgrade makes your *next* run meaningfully faster or richer, it is probably worth buying now. If it only matters twenty loops from now, it can wait until your income supports it.

Also resist the urge to buy every cheap node just because you can afford it. In most incremental games, the cheapest upgrade is rarely the best value — it is just the one the UI puts in front of you.

## Permanent Upgrade Categories Compared

Not all permanent growth is created equal. Broadly, Loop Dungeon Incremental permanent upgrades fall into a handful of categories, each with a different return curve and a different moment when it shines.

| Category | What it improves | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Combat power | Damage, health, survivability | Breaking through a floor wall | Diminishing returns if you ignore economy |
| Economy | Loot and resource gain per run | Building momentum early | Useless if your party cannot clear floors |
| Roster and party | Hero slots, recruitment, flexibility | Mid-game synergy builds | Expensive before your income is stable |
| Convenience | Automation, speed, offline gains | Busy or casual players | Does not raise your ceiling on its own |
| Unlocks | New heroes, paths, and mechanics | Long-term players | Slow payoff; easy to over-invest too early |

The healthiest progression mixes two categories at a time: one that raises your ceiling and one that raises your income. Pure combat investment stalls when you cannot afford the next tier. Pure economy investment stalls when your heroes cannot survive long enough to spend it.

### Pairing Upgrades With Your Party

Your roster is a multiplier on everything else. Community reports and the game's own design notes both emphasize synergies between characters and upgrades — so buy permanent upgrades that support the roles you actually field, not the ones a generic guide tells you to field.

| Role | Job in the loop | Permanent upgrades that support it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frontline bruiser | Soaks damage and holds the line | Health, defense, healing-over-time effects |
| Damage dealer | Clears floors quickly | Attack, critical chance, action speed |
| Support or healer | Keeps the party alive through long loops | Healing output, cooldown reduction |
| Utility flex | Enables synergies between other heroes | Roster unlocks, party slots, flexibility nodes |

If you are running a glass-cannon composition, survivability upgrades are not optional — they are what lets your damage actually land. If you are running a slow, tanky squad, invest in speed and economy so your runs do not take forever.

## A Loop-by-Loop Spending Plan

Here is a concrete plan you can follow for your first several resets. Adjust the loop numbers to your own pace — the order matters more than the exact count.

| Loops | Focus | Target outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–3 | Cheap combat and economy nodes | Consistent clears without stalling |
| 4–8 | Income multipliers | Noticeably more resources per run |
| 9–15 | Party slots and role depth | A squad that can handle longer loops |
| 15–25 | Prestige bonuses | Each reset starts stronger than the last |
| 25+ | Unlock paths and specialization | New strategies and deeper floors |

Three habits separate fast progress from slow progress:

- **Reset on a plateau, not on a whim.** If your last three runs ended at roughly the same depth, you are done with this loop.
- **Spend before you reset.** Unspent currency sitting in your bank is the most common waste in incremental games.
- **Track one metric.** Pick floors cleared or resources per hour and watch it. If an upgrade does not move that number, it was the wrong purchase.

## FAQ

**Do Loop Dungeon Incremental permanent upgrades carry over after a reset?**
Yes. Permanent upgrades and prestige bonuses are designed to persist across resets — that is the entire point of the meta-progression layer. Your current floor depth and run-based hero levels do not carry over, so spend everything useful before you reset.

**What should I buy first with my early currency?**
Start with cheap combat and economy nodes that let you clear floors consistently. Once clears are stable, shift into income multipliers and then party slots. Loop Dungeon Incremental permanent upgrades compound, so the earlier you start a multiplier, the more value it returns.

**How often should I prestige?**
When your runs stop reaching new floors. If three consecutive expeditions end at the same depth, the current loop has given you everything it has — reset, spend, and climb again. Prestiging early is almost always better than grinding a wall.

**Are these upgrades worth it if I only play casually?**
Yes, and the game is built for it. Automatic combat and prestige bonuses mean progress continues whether you are actively optimizing or checking in periodically. Prioritize offline and convenience upgrades if your sessions are short, then let the multipliers do the work between visits.

The bottom line: Loop Dungeon Incremental permanent upgrades are not a side system — they are the main character. Buy the ones that make your next run faster, reset the moment progress flattens, and let prestige bonuses turn a hundred small loops into one very long climb.
