## Your First Hour: A Step-by-Step Opening Plan

The opening hour sets your tempo for the next ten. Don't freestyle it.

| Step | Action | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Run the dungeon with your starting party, no upgrades | Learn the pacing and see where the party stalls |
| 2 | Recruit a second hero before buying damage upgrades | A wider roster beats a slightly stronger one early |
| 3 | Fill your front line so damage isn't funneled onto one hero | Survivability compounds faster than raw DPS at low levels |
| 4 | Buy cheap, permanent upgrades first | Flat, always-on bonuses never become dead weight |
| 5 | Push until your party wipes twice at the same floor | That's your signal to invest, not to reset |
| 6 | Bank loot until you can afford a meaningful jump | Ten small purchases rarely beat one tier upgrade |

The single most common early error is buying every affordable upgrade the moment it appears. Cheap upgrades feel productive, but they dilute your resources across stats that don't multiply each other. The Loop Dungeon Incremental beginner guide checklist above exists to slow you down just enough to make that jump.

Also: let the game idle. Automatic combat means progress continues while you're doing something else, and incremental games are designed around that. Checking in every few minutes to re-spend currency is fine; staring at the screen is not required.

## Party Building: Roles, Balance, and Synergies

The game's own description emphasizes recruiting heroes with different strengths and roles, then discovering synergies between characters and upgrades. Translation: a party of four damage dealers will lose to a balanced party of three.

| Role | Job | What Happens If You Skip It |
|---|---|---|
| Frontline / tank | Soaks hits, protects the back line | Squishy heroes die first, damage collapses |
| Primary damage | Kills enemies before they act | Fights drag on and chip damage adds up |
| Support / healer | Sustains the party across floors | You clear early floors, then stall hard |
| Utility / flex | Buffs, debuffs, or synergy enabler | Your party has no multiplier to scale with |

Three rules cover most of it:

- **Keep a frontline body between your damage dealers and the enemy.** This is the highest-value structural decision in the game.
- **Chase synergies, not raw stats.** A modest upgrade that amplifies your whole party usually beats a big upgrade that helps one hero.
- **Rotate heroes instead of replacing them.** Heroes that don't fit your current depth may fit the next one; incremental games reward a deep bench.

If you remember nothing else from this Loop Dungeon Incremental beginner guide, remember this: your party is a system, not a leaderboard. The hero with the biggest number is rarely the reason a run succeeds.

## Understanding the Loop: Runs, Resets, and Prestige

Every expedition feeds long-term growth, and even a reset leaves you stronger through prestige bonuses. That is the genre promise, and it is also where new players get impatient.

| Signal | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Party clears a new floor comfortably | You're under-invested in depth | Push two or three floors further |
| Party wipes instantly on a new floor | Wall, not a failure | Farm the previous floor for loot |
| Upgrades cost 5–10x your current income | Diminishing returns have started | Consider a prestige reset |
| Prestige bonus would be a large multiplier | Reset is clearly worth it | Take the reset |
| You just reset five minutes ago | You're resetting on emotion | Go back and push deeper |

A Loop Dungeon Incremental beginner guide that skips prestige would be incomplete, because prestige is the actual engine of the game. The general rule across incrementals applies here: reset when the next upgrade costs dramatically more than your income supports, not when you get bored. Each reset should land you deeper than the last, faster than the last.

## Where to Spend: Upgrade Priorities That Hold Up

Not all upgrades are equal, and the difference widens as you go deeper. Here's a sensible priority ladder for your first several hours.

| Priority | Upgrade Type | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Party-wide multipliers | Scales with every hero you'll ever recruit |
| 2 | Survivability for the frontline | Keeps runs going long enough to matter |
| 3 | Recruitment and roster depth | More options means better synergy hunting |
| 4 | Loot and resource gain | Compounds everything else you buy |
| 5 | Single-hero stat boosts | Fine, but only after the above |
| 6 | Cosmetic or convenience purchases | Last, always |

Community reports from incremental players consistently point to the same pattern: broad multipliers first, narrow boosts last. If two upgrades cost the same and one affects the entire party, that's your answer almost every time.

## FAQ

**Is Loop Dungeon Incremental hard for someone new to incrementals?**
No. Combat is automatic and the systems reveal themselves gradually. This Loop Dungeon Incremental beginner guide covers everything the tutorial skips, and the free demo lets you learn the loop without spending anything.

**How often should I prestige?**
Only when the next meaningful upgrade costs far more than your current income supports, or when the prestige multiplier would clearly jump you several floors. Resetting because you're impatient usually costs you progress.

**Do I need a perfectly balanced party?**
No, but you do need a frontline, a damage source, and some form of sustain. Perfect synergy comes later; functional structure comes first.

**What should I buy first?**
Party-wide multipliers and frontline survivability. Single-hero stat boosts are the last thing you should spend on, even when they look tempting.

**Does the game run on low-end PCs?**
Yes — the listed minimum is Windows 10 with integrated graphics and 500 MB of storage. Just be aware of the documented Windows 11 startup issue mentioned earlier.
